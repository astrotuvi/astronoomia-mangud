# Game ideas

Ideas for new games, not built yet. For how a new game should look and
work, see [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

These ideas have already become games: Tähtkujud, Mälumäng, Kosmoserakett,
Kuu faasid, Hüppa planeedil, Püüa langev täht, Kosmoseviktoriin, Planeetide
rivi, Avasta eksoplaneet and Leia asteroid.

## Ages 5–6

1. **Leia erinevus (spot the difference):** two copies of a club photo,
   where a star, the Moon or a satellite trail is added or removed in one.
2. **Varjumäng (shadow match):** drag Saturn, the Moon, a rocket or a
   galaxy onto its dark outline. It teaches shapes such as Saturn's rings
   and a galaxy's spiral.
3. **Päev või öö (day or night):** sort pictures into a day sky or a night
   sky: the Sun, stars, an owl, a rainbow, the northern lights. The Moon
   can go in both.
4. **Maandumine (Moon landing):** hold one button to fire the engine and
   land gently, with forgiving physics.
5. **Riieta astronaut (dress the astronaut):** put on the helmet, suit,
   gloves, boots and backpack. Each part says what it's for, e.g. "the
   helmet gives air to breathe", and then the astronaut floats out of the
   airlock.
6. **Mis see on? (sort the sky):** drag the club photos into baskets for
   planet, star cluster, nebula, galaxy and Moon, with a short fact for
   each.
7. **Kraatrimeister (crater maker):** flick rocks at a bare Moon to make
   craters. Bigger, faster rocks make bigger craters with rays. At the end
   the picture turns into a real photo of the Moon.

## Ages 8–10

8. **Kosmosesond (space probe):** set the launch angle and speed so that a
   probe reaches a planet, with a slingshot past Jupiter in later levels.
   A wrong guess shows a curved path, so failing is fun too. It could
   reuse the Kepler orbits from Kosmoserakett.
9. **Marsikulgur (Mars rover):** plan a route of moves ("forward 3, turn
   left, forward 2") on a grid to reach rocks or ice, then press go.
   Signals take minutes to reach Mars, so the rover can't be steered live.
   It's a first taste of coding.
10. **Taevakaart (star hopping):** find stars by following lines from ones
    you know, such as from Suur Vanker to Põhjanael, or along Orion's belt
    to Sirius. It reuses the star positions from Tähtkujud.
11. **Päev ja öö, aastaajad (day, night and seasons):** spin and tilt the
    Earth with Estonia marked on it, and move it round the Sun through the
    year. Tasks: "Make it noon in Estonia", "Find the shortest day", "Find
    where the Sun doesn't set". It shows why it's dark at 15:30 in December
    and light at midnight in June, and goes well with Kuu faasid.
12. **Päikesekell (shadows and sundial):** drag the Sun across the sky over
    a stick, a tree or a child, and watch the shadow turn and change
    length. Tasks ask for the shortest shadow (noon) or one pointing east,
    and then the time is read from the shadow.
13. **Teleskoobiga taevas (through the telescope):** pan a small telescope
    view across a big night sky to find targets such as Saturn or the
    Orion Nebula. When one is found, its photo zooms in.
14. **Päikesesüsteemi jalutuskäik (walk through the Solar System):** scroll
    from the Sun to Neptune with sizes and distances to scale, with a step
    counter or "days at rocket speed", to show how empty space is.
15. **Satelliidi start (launch a satellite):** throw a satellite sideways.
    Too slow and it falls back, too fast and it flies away, just right and
    it orbits. Level 2 does the same on the Moon.
16. **Raketi ehitaja 2 (rocket builder 2):** a harder Kosmoserakett, with a
    budget and a choice of engines and tanks. The rocket must carry enough
    fuel without getting too heavy.

## Ages 11–12 and harder

17. **Kui kaugel on täht? (how far is the star?):** find a pulsating star's
    (a Cepheid's) period from its light curve, read its true brightness
    off a period–brightness line, and compare it with how bright it looks
    to get its distance. Harder levels use a Cepheid in another galaxy. It
    reuses the graph, zoom and ruler from Avasta eksoplaneet.
18. **Kaksiktähed (binary stars):** a light curve with two dips of
    different depths, where two stars eclipse each other. The player works
    out which star is hotter, which is bigger, and the period, and learns
    to tell such a curve from a planet's.
19. **Tähe spekter (a star's spectrum):** match absorption lines to the
    "barcodes" of hydrogen, helium, sodium and iron to find what a star is
    made of. Then use the lines' wobble to find a planet, and end with a
    galaxy whose lines are all shifted red, leading to the Hubble law.
20. **Kaalu planeeti (weigh a planet):** find Jupiter's moons as dots that
    change sides from night to night, as Galileo did, time them and
    measure their orbits. A bigger orbit means a longer year (Kepler's
    third law), which is used to compare the planets' masses.
21. **Lend planeedile (plan the flight):** pick the launch date so that the
    planet is in the right place when the rocket arrives, with a fuel
    limit. Harder levels swing past Jupiter for a gravity assist to reach
    Saturn.
22. **Tähe elu (life of a star):** place stars on a colour–brightness
    diagram (the HR diagram), then pick a star's mass and predict whether
    it ends as a white dwarf, a neutron star or a black hole.

## New nights for Püüa langev täht

- **The Leonids in November:** the view faces east as Lõvi rises after
  midnight. Level 3's meteor storm would suit it better than the
  Perseids, as the Leonids have given real storms (1833, 1966, 1999–2001).
- **The Quadrantids in January:** easier to add, as the view faces
  north-east like the August night. The peak lasts only a few hours.
