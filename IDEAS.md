# Game ideas

Ideas for new games, not built yet. For how a new game should look and
work, see [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

These ideas have already become games: Tähtkujud, Mälumäng, Kosmoserakett,
Kuu faasid, Hüppa planeedil, Püüa langev täht, Kosmoseviktoriin, Planeetide
rivi, Avasta eksoplaneet, Leia asteroid and Uuri meteoore.

## Ages 5–6

1. **Leia erinevus (spot the difference):** two copies of a club photo,
   where a star, the Moon or a satellite trail is added or removed in one.
2. **Varjumäng (shadow match):** drag Saturn, the Moon, a rocket or a
   galaxy onto its dark outline. It teaches shapes such as Saturn's rings
   and a galaxy's spiral.
3. **Päev või öö (day or night):** sort pictures into a day sky or a night
   sky: the Sun, stars, an owl, a rainbow, the northern lights. The Moon
   can go in both.
4. **Maandumine (Moon landing):** hold one button to fire the engine and
   land gently, with forgiving physics.
5. **Riieta astronaut (dress the astronaut):** put on the helmet, suit,
   gloves, boots and backpack. Each part says what it's for, e.g. "the
   helmet gives air to breathe", and then the astronaut floats out of the
   airlock.
6. **Mis see on? (sort the sky):** drag the club photos into baskets for
   planet, star cluster, nebula, galaxy and Moon, with a short fact for
   each.
7. **Kraatrimeister (crater maker):** flick rocks at a bare Moon to make
   craters. Bigger, faster rocks make bigger craters with rays. At the end
   the picture turns into a real photo of the Moon.
8. **Pulsari rütm (pulsar rhythm):** a spinning star sends out clicks,
   and the child taps along with them. Later levels play two or three
   pulsars, and the child tells them apart by their rhythm. It needs no
   reading, and the clicks are made in code.
9. **Komeedi saba (comet's tail):** drag a comet round the Sun. Its tail
   grows near the Sun and always points away from it, even when the
   comet is moving away from the Sun.
10. **Kosmoseprügi (space junk):** catch old satellite parts with a net
    before they hit each other and break into more pieces. It could reuse
    the satellites from Püüa langev täht.
11. **Minu tähtkuju (my constellation):** join any stars into a figure of
    one's own and give it a name, then see what the Greeks or the old
    Estonians saw there. It reuses the stars from Tähtkujud.

## Ages 8–10

12. **Kosmosesond (space probe):** set the launch angle and speed so that a
   probe reaches a planet, with a slingshot past Jupiter in later levels.
   A wrong guess shows a curved path, so failing is fun too. It could
   reuse the Kepler orbits from Kosmoserakett.
13. **Marsikulgur (Mars rover):** plan a route of moves ("forward 3, turn
   left, forward 2") on a grid to reach rocks or ice, then press go.
   Signals take minutes to reach Mars, so the rover can't be steered live.
   It's a first taste of coding.
14. **Taevakaart (star hopping):** find stars by following lines from ones
    you know, such as from Suur Vanker to Põhjanael, or along Orion's belt
    to Sirius. It reuses the star positions from Tähtkujud.
15. **Päev ja öö, aastaajad (day, night and seasons):** spin and tilt the
    Earth with Estonia marked on it, and move it round the Sun through the
    year. Tasks: "Make it noon in Estonia", "Find the shortest day", "Find
    where the Sun doesn't set". It shows why it's dark at 15:30 in December
    and light at midnight in June, and goes well with Kuu faasid. See
    [IDEAS-seasons.md](IDEAS-seasons.md) for the levels, titled "Kuidas
    tekivad aastaajad?", and what the game needs.
16. **Päikesekell (shadows and sundial):** drag the Sun across the sky over
    a stick, a tree or a child, and watch the shadow turn and change
    length. Tasks ask for the shortest shadow (noon) or one pointing east,
    and then the time is read from the shadow.
17. **Teleskoobiga taevas (through the telescope):** pan a small telescope
    view across a big night sky to find targets such as Saturn or the
    Orion Nebula. When one is found, its photo zooms in.
18. **Päikesesüsteemi jalutuskäik (walk through the Solar System):** scroll
    from the Sun to Neptune with sizes and distances to scale, with a step
    counter or "days at rocket speed", to show how empty space is.
19. **Satelliidi start (launch a satellite):** throw a satellite sideways.
    Too slow and it falls back, too fast and it flies away, just right and
    it orbits. Level 2 does the same on the Moon.
20. **Raketi ehitaja 2 (rocket builder 2):** a harder Kosmoserakett, with a
    budget and a choice of engines and tanks. The rocket must carry enough
    fuel without getting too heavy.
21. **Virmalised (northern lights):** guide particles from the Sun along
    the Earth's magnetic field to the poles. Low ones glow green and high
    ones red. Auroras were often seen from Estonia in 2024–2025.
22. **Pime taevas (dark sky):** a town at night under a sky washed out by
    street lights. Shield or switch off the lamps, and more and more stars
    appear, until the Milky Way comes out.
23. **Tähejäljed (star trails):** in a long-exposure photo the stars draw
    arcs. Find Põhjanael at their centre, then set how long the camera
    was open to get a quarter or a half circle. It goes well with Uuri
    meteoore.
24. **Rahvapärased tähenimed (folk star names):** match old Estonian names
    to the sky, such as Sõel, Kolm kuningat (Orion's belt) and Linnutee,
    the birds' road, with a short tale for each. It reuses the star
    positions from Tähtkujud.
25. **ESTCube:** assemble Estonia's first satellite, launch it and unfold
    its electric solar sail, and then follow ESTCube-2.
26. **Asteroidi kaitse (planetary defence):** push an asteroid off course,
    as NASA's DART did. A small push years ahead is enough, a push at the
    last moment is too late. It follows on from Leia asteroid.

## Ages 11–12

27. **Kui kaugel on täht? (how far is the star?):** find a pulsating star's
    (a Cepheid's) period from its light curve, read its true brightness
    off a period–brightness line, and compare it with how bright it looks
    to get its distance. Harder levels use a Cepheid in another galaxy. It
    reuses the graph, zoom and ruler from Avasta eksoplaneet.
28. **Kaksiktähed (binary stars):** a light curve with two dips of
    different depths, where two stars eclipse each other. The player works
    out which star is hotter, which is bigger, and the period, and learns
    to tell such a curve from a planet's.
29. **Tähe spekter (a star's spectrum):** match absorption lines to the
    "barcodes" of hydrogen, helium, sodium and iron to find what a star is
    made of. Then use the lines' wobble to find a planet, and end with a
    galaxy whose lines are all shifted red, leading to the Hubble law.
30. **Kaalu planeeti (weigh a planet):** find Jupiter's moons as dots that
    change sides from night to night, as Galileo did, time them and
    measure their orbits. A bigger orbit means a longer year (Kepler's
    third law), which is used to compare the planets' masses.
31. **Lend planeedile (plan the flight):** pick the launch date so that the
    planet is in the right place when the rocket arrives, with a fuel
    limit. Harder levels swing past Jupiter for a gravity assist to reach
    Saturn.
32. **Tähe elu (life of a star):** place stars on a colour–brightness
    diagram (the HR diagram), then pick a star's mass and predict whether
    it ends as a white dwarf, a neutron star or a black hole.
33. **Parallaks (parallax):** look at a thumb with one eye and then the
    other, then at a nearby star from the two sides of the Earth's orbit,
    half a year apart, and work out how far it is. It leads into Kui
    kaugel on täht?.
34. **Maa mõõtmine (measuring the Earth):** find the Earth's size from noon
    shadows in two towns, as Eratosthenes did, and then follow the Struve
    Geodetic Arc, which crosses Estonia and is a UNESCO site.
35. **Ehita teleskoop (build a telescope):** place two lenses so that the
    light comes to a focus, and see how their focal lengths set the
    magnification. Galileo's and Newton's telescopes could be levels.
36. **Elamiskõlblik vöönd (habitable zone):** place a planet round a red,
    yellow or blue star where its water is liquid, not ice or steam. It
    goes well with Avasta eksoplaneet.

## Teenagers and adults

37. **Kosmiline võrk (cosmic web):** galaxies lie along filaments and walls
    that meet in clusters, with huge empty voids between them. The player
    finds this pattern in real SDSS galaxy data, as astronomers did, by
    adding distances to the dots and tracing the filaments and voids, and
    then builds a universe where gravity and dark matter make the web.
    The web was found by Jaan Einasto's group in Tartu in the 1970s. See
    [IDEAS-cosmicweb.md](IDEAS-cosmicweb.md) for the levels and what the
    game needs.
38. **Dokkimine (docking):** dock with the space station. To catch up with
    it, first slow down, as a lower orbit is faster. It could reuse the
    Kepler orbits from Kosmoserakett.
39. **Must auk (black hole):** send light and things past a black hole and
    watch the light bend, time slow down and things get stretched.

## New nights for Püüa langev täht

- **The Leonids in November:** the view faces east as Lõvi rises after
  midnight. Level 3's meteor storm would suit it better than the
  Perseids, as the Leonids have given real storms (1833, 1966, 1999–2001).
- **The Quadrantids in January:** easier to add, as the view faces
  north-east like the August night. The peak lasts only a few hours.
